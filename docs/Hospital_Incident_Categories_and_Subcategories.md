# Hospital Incident Categories and Subcategories

## Purpose

This master list is intended for an incident-reporting application used in a large multispecialty hospital. The reporter should select one **category** and one **subcategory** for each incident. More than one category may be linked when an incident crosses departments.

> **Important:** A clinical complication or poor outcome is not automatically an error. Events such as cardiac arrest, unexpected death, unplanned ICU transfer, or maternal complication should trigger review to determine whether an unsafe act, omission, system failure, or unavoidable clinical condition was involved.

## Suggested Harm Classification

1. **Near miss:** The incident did not reach the person.
2. **No harm:** The incident reached the person but caused no detectable harm.
3. **Mild harm:** Temporary harm requiring minimal intervention.
4. **Moderate harm:** Temporary harm requiring additional treatment, prolonged stay, or increased monitoring.
5. **Severe harm:** Permanent harm, major intervention, or life-sustaining treatment required.
6. **Death:** The incident caused or contributed to death.

## Configuration Notes

- Keep **medication errors** separate from **adverse drug reactions**.
- Permit confidential or restricted routing for safeguarding, sexual harassment, self-harm, cybersecurity, and medico-legal incidents.
- Allow the quality team to reclassify an incident after review while preserving the original selection in the audit trail.
- Configure department-specific subcategories only for services available in the hospital.
- Maintain version control for the category master and never delete historical values already linked to submitted reports.
